# Site Copy Audit — Paradigm Shift Software Development

**Date:** 2026-07-18
**Scope:** all marketing copy — Landing, Automated-Workflows index, the 9 workflow
detail pages, and Technical Consulting.
**Lens:** conversion copywriting — problem-first, customer's-world specificity,
one clear voice, objection handling, benefit over feature.

---

## The one-line finding

**Your automation copy is genuinely excellent. Your consulting copy is generic —
and the two don't sound like the same person.** The fastest, highest-impact work
is not writing new copy; it's (1) unifying the *voice*, (2) fixing a handful of
errors, and (3) pulling the Technical Consulting page up to the standard the
workflow pages already set.

---

## What's already strong (preserve — do not "improve" these)

- **Workflow detail headlines.** Problem-first, concrete, in the customer's world:
  - "The work is finished. The money is still sitting with the customer."
  - "…an emergency comes in before you've finished your coffee…"
  - "…books the job while you're still up a ladder."
  These are conversion-grade. Don't touch them.
- **Workflow FAQs.** They handle objections honestly and even push back
  ("If your gut says your customers would resent this, take that seriously").
  This builds more trust than any testimonial. Keep this voice everywhere.
- **The boundaries line:** "No hourly billing. No open-ended consulting. No
  surprise scope creep." Strong. Reuse the pattern.
- **"CTO-level judgment without CTO-level commitment."** Best line on the
  consulting page. Build around it.
- **Demo honesty** ("on a made-up business") + the new "Illustrative" tags.

## P0 — Site-wide (fast, high impact)

### 1. Pick ONE voice: "I", not "we"

You're a solo operator, and the strongest copy on the site already says "I"
("I'm a software engineer with 10+ years…", "I design and build…"). But the
**Technical Consulting page switches to "we / us / our"** — same person, different
pronoun. To a reader this either implies a team that doesn't exist (misleading) or
reads as careless. "I" is also warmer and more credible for a founder-to-founder
sale.

| Where | Now | Change to |
|---|---|---|
| WhyWorkWithMe title | "Why Work With **Us**" | "Why work with **me**" |
| WhyWorkWithMe intro | "**We specialize** in helping founders…" | "**I help** founders who don't want to make these decisions alone." |
| WhyWorkWithMe focus | "**Out** focus is not writing code…" | "**My** focus isn't writing code…" |
| Problem title | "The Problem **We** Help You Solve" | "The problem **I** help you solve" |
| HowItWorks steps | "**We** start with…", "**we'll** confirm…" | "**I** start with…", "**I'll** confirm…" |
| CTA description | "**We'll** determine whether…" | "**I'll** help you figure out whether…" |

### 2. Fix the outright errors (do these today)

- **Typo:** WhyWorkWithMe — "**Out** focus is not writing code" → "**My** focus".
- **Price formatting:** Offer — "$4,000 - $6000" → "**$4,000–$6,000**" (missing
  comma + use the en-dash you already use in "2–4 weeks").
- **Capitalization style:** the consulting page is Title Case ("Who This Is For",
  "Choose Your Path", "How It Works"), the rest of the site is sentence case
  ("A few examples of what that looks like in practice"). Pick one — recommend
  **sentence case everywhere** to match the (stronger) automation pages.

### 3. Resolve the audience contradiction on WhoIsFor

> "You're building a **B2C SaaS product** and need to make early technical
> decisions…"

Everything else on the consulting side targets **non-technical founders**
broadly (and the landing says "growing businesses"). "B2C SaaS product" silently
narrows — and excludes — most of that audience. Either commit to that niche
across the whole page (if it's your real ICP) or broaden it:

> **Before:** "You're building a B2C SaaS product and need to make early
> technical decisions…"
> **After:** "You've got a product idea — or an early build — and you need to
> make technical decisions you can't fully evaluate yet, without overbuilding or
> guessing wrong."

## P1 — Technical Consulting page (bring it up to the automation standard)

The *structure* is fine (clear tiers, prices, outcomes). The *prose* is generic —
it "tells" ("focused engagement", "actionable guidance tailored to your specific
situation — no generic advice") where the workflow pages "show". Apply the same
problem-first, concrete treatment.

- **Hero** is acceptable ("Technical clarity for non-technical founders" is a real
  positioning line). Just lowercase it and keep the subhead.
- **Problem section** is good structurally (the four founder questions are
  concrete). Keep it; just fix the title voice/case.
- **Offer "What it is / Outcome" copy** — sharpen from abstract to concrete. Example:

  | Tier | Now (abstract) | Sharper (concrete) |
  |---|---|---|
  | MVP Tech Blueprint · *What it is* | "A focused engagement that gives you a clear, opinionated technical plan before you write code or hire engineers." | "A written, opinionated plan for what to build first, what to build it with, and what to skip — so your first dollar and first hire go to the right thing." |
  | Fractional CTO · *What it is* | "Lightweight, high-level technical leadership… without bringing on a full-time CTO." | "A technical partner on call for the decisions that are too big to guess on — hiring, architecture, vendor and build-vs-buy calls — without a full-time CTO's salary or equity." |

- **WhyWorkWithMe benefits** ("Move faster with confidence / Avoid expensive
  rework / Speak credibly with developers and investors") are good — keep, just fix
  the voice + typo above.

## P1 — Landing page hero (the weakest positioning on the site)

> **Now:** "Software expertise for growing businesses" /
> "From early-stage technical strategy to automating the work that slows you down."

"Software expertise" is inside-out (about you) and vague. The subhead is actually
the stronger line and already bridges both service lines. Lead with outcome:

- **Option A (clarity/outcome):** "Make the right technical calls — and automate
  the work that's eating your week."
- **Option B (bridge, keeps both ICPs):** "Technical strategy for founders,
  automation for the businesses already running." *(subhead: what you have now)*
- **Option C (keep it simple):** promote the current subhead to the H1 and cut
  "Software expertise for growing businesses" entirely.

Recommend A. Keep the two service cards doing the routing work below.

---

## P2 — Smaller sharpening

- **Automation index subhead** leads with credentials ("I'm a software engineer
  with 10+ years…") before the benefit. Consider flipping: hook with the outcome,
  then earn it with the credential. (Low priority — it reads fine.)
- **CTA labels** vary by segment ("Book a free automation consult" vs "Book a
  founder call") — that's *good*, keep segment-specific. But the header's generic
  "Book a call" could inherit the page's segment label where feasible.
- **"What we do"** (landing services heading) → "What I do" to match the unified
  voice, or leave as the one acceptable neutral use.

## Suggested order of work

1. **P0 quick wins** (typo, price format, voice `we→I`, capitalization) — an hour,
   big cohesion gain, zero risk.
2. **WhoIsFor audience line** — resolve the B2C-SaaS contradiction.
3. **Landing hero H1** — pick an option, sharpen positioning.
4. **Consulting Offer/section prose** — concrete rewrites, page by page.
5. Leave the workflow detail copy alone — it's the model everything else should
   match.
