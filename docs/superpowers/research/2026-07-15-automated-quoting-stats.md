## Outcome

**Zero figures accepted, nine evaluated and rejected. This workflow ships `stats: []`.**

The plan classified this workflow as "uncertain — research honestly and accept the result." The honest result is that no publishable statistic exists. Every candidate failed at least one acceptance criterion, and the overwhelming majority failed criterion 1 (primary or near-primary): the quoting-speed statistics that saturate this space are published by companies selling quoting, answering, or automation software — including, in one case, an AI automation consultancy selling the exact service this page sells.

Per the spec's rule — **a stats section is cut, never padded** — fewer than two survivors means no stats section. No apology required.

## Assessment of the evidence base

**The sibling workflow's conclusion generalizes, and the mechanism is now clearer.** Missed-call text-back and automated quoting draw on the same polluted well. Four things a later task should know:

1. **There is no primary research on quote turnaround and job win rates for small trades. Not "hard to find" — it does not exist.** I searched academic field-experiment literature, peer-reviewed operations/marketing journals, NBER, construction-management bidding literature, US government statistics (Census/BLS), Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies, and independent consumer organizations (Which?, Consumer Reports). None of them has studied this question. The commercial incentive to *assert* the answer is enormous; the academic incentive to *measure* it is nil. That asymmetry is why the space looks data-rich and is actually empty.

2. **The characteristic failure here is population swap, not fabrication.** This differs from the sibling workflow, and it is worth internalizing. The sibling found invented studies (the non-existent "2024 Pew voicemail survey," the non-existent "BrightLocal Local Consumer Communication Study 2025" — both confirmed fabrications from `ainora.lt` / `hicira.com`). **I found no new fabricated institutional citations in this workflow.** What I found instead is subtler and easier to miss: real-ish claims whose *population* is quietly rewritten in transit. "78% of **B2B customers** buy from the vendor that responds first" becomes "78% of **homeowners** hire the first contractor who responds." Nobody invented a study; they changed who it was about. A reviewer checking only "does a source exist?" would pass this. Criterion 4 is what catches it — apply it literally.

3. **Trade publications are the only near-primary sources in this space, and they don't publish methodology.** Roofing Contractor and ACHR News run their own homeowner surveys — not vendor marketing, a real distinction that made them the strongest candidates found. Both fail anyway: RC discloses no sample size or method at all, and ACHR is behind a 403. If someone later wants a stats section badly enough to revisit this, RC's promised "full survey report in a future edition" is the only thread worth pulling — and it would still be roofing, not HVAC/plumbing/cleaning.

4. **Every "time spent quoting" figure is published by someone selling quote automation.** This was the brief's third requested angle and it is the most conflicted of the three. The best-fitting candidate (HeyBRB's "20 minutes per quote") is published by an AI automation consultancy — the same business model as PDS. Citing a competitor's self-serving survey to sell the same service is the worst available outcome for credibility, not the best.

**Recommendation:** ship `stats: []`. The argument for automated quoting is mechanical and self-evident to the reader: a trades owner knows what it costs to write quotes at 9pm and knows what happens when a quote lands three days late — they live it. A borrowed number about B2B software buyers or commercial sealed-bid tenders would make the page *less* credible to that reader, because they would recognize immediately that it isn't about them. If a stats section is wanted later, the only defensible source is first-party data from PDS's own deployments, published and labeled as such.
